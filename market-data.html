Office rents, market by market, without the spin

Office headlines spent years announcing the death of the workplace, then spent 2025 quietly revising that story in a handful of cities. The national vacancy rate finally ticked down, Manhattan leased more space than it had in years, and San Francisco — still the emptiest major market — posted its largest annual vacancy decline since 2011. The recovery is real in places and absent in others, and the rent figures below keep those two facts separate.

Two Manhattan numbers deserve a footnote, not a correction: CBRE's $77.89 and CommercialSearch's $68.15 are different series — CBRE's average asking rent for its Manhattan market definition against a full-service equivalent listing rate — so they should not be averaged or treated as a discrepancy. The same applies to vacancy: 12.7% (CBRE, December 2025) and 13.6% (CommercialSearch, December 2025) differ by definition and sample, not by error. What both series agree on is direction: Manhattan tightened, San Francisco improved from an extreme, and the national market stabilized rather than recovered.

Methodology

Included: CBRE Research market reports (Manhattan Q4 2025, U.S. Office Q3 2025, Tech Gateway) and CommercialSearch's December 2025 national report, with series differences labeled. Excluded: JLL and Cushman & Wakefield figures (not verified this session), achieved/taking rents except where CBRE's taking-rent index is noted in the source, and submarket rents beyond the Manhattan total. Asking rents are landlord asks, not signed rents.
